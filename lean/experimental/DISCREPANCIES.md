## COL-718 unequal impulse limits

Two touching 1 kg bodies approach with normal velocities −100 and −90 m/s. The raw exchange would add +10 and −10 m/s. With maximum speeds 1 and 2 m/s, the first body retains one quarter of its kick and the second retains all of it. The stored results are −97.5 and −100 m/s. In the original frame kinetic energy rises by 703.125 J. Adding 100 m/s to every velocity gives a loss of 46.875 J for the same event. The internal kinetic energy, computed about each state’s own centre of mass, falls by 23.4375 J. This example uses exactly representable limits. [Frame theorems](../ExactBallparkProofs/Physics/ContactFrames.lean) · [Scalar replay](../scripts/boundary_checks.py) · [Compiled pair replay](../scripts/contact_replay.py)

**Change needed to conserve pair momentum.** Apply one common impulse to both bodies, using their base masses to convert it to their two velocity changes. If both existing caps must hold, a shared retained fraction can dissipate energy or leave a pair still approaching. In this example the tighter cap permits a fraction of ¼ and separation requires at least ½. A fully elastic separating response requires the uncapped impulse. [Real impulse and limit conditions](../ExactBallparkProofs/Collision/LimitedPair.lean) · [Scalar limiter](https://github.com/Eidosoma/exact-ballpark/blob/f34c1d14fc2c234d353bdb2578872cdb0478d19e/src/exact_ballpark/space.py#L1942-L1952) · [Destiny limiter](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/114e89fa584f0e1a81eb0a679aabf41af8870072/src/Ballpark.cpp#L2590-L2603)

## COL-715 repeat collisions

The sweep returns a fraction `u`. The repeat guard compares it with the stored value `1e-4`. A subsequent history scan subtracts that same value from a timestamp in seconds. Under an elapsed-time interpretation the first comparison needs `u × segment duration`. For a two-second sweep the raw fraction gate covers 200 µs, while for a half-second sweep it covers 50 µs. Equality is included. [Scalar guard and history scan](https://github.com/Eidosoma/exact-ballpark/blob/f34c1d14fc2c234d353bdb2578872cdb0478d19e/src/exact_ballpark/space.py#L475-L485) · [Destiny guard](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/114e89fa584f0e1a81eb0a679aabf41af8870072/src/CollisionBallProperties.cpp#L198-L214)

The fixture gives a ship a 100 km straight sweep and seeds its starting history record with collider 7. On the scalar fixed-obstacle path the engine and control make opposite candidate selections at two durations. At one second they agree below, at and above the cutoff. The compiled C++ replay exercises the original sweep, history constructor and repeat predicate on the same inputs. This evidence ends at candidate selection. An effect on the final ship state would require a reachable contact history and an end-of-tick replay. [Lean fraction and time comparison](RealPhysics/TimeGuards.lean) · [Replay decisions](RESULTS.md#repeat-collision-decisions)

**Change needed to adopt this interpretation.** Supply elapsed seconds to the first guard at the shape, fixed-ball and free-ball callers in both implementations. For a free-ball subsegment, use its own `tend − tstart`. A policy measuring time since the previous same-collider impact would need that impact’s timestamp and the candidate’s absolute time. The two policies can differ when a search is subdivided. [Scalar fixed and free callers](https://github.com/Eidosoma/exact-ballpark/blob/f34c1d14fc2c234d353bdb2578872cdb0478d19e/src/exact_ballpark/space.py#L1828-L1872) · [Scalar shape caller](https://github.com/Eidosoma/exact-ballpark/blob/f34c1d14fc2c234d353bdb2578872cdb0478d19e/src/exact_ballpark/full.py#L2954-L2964) · [Destiny callers](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/114e89fa584f0e1a81eb0a679aabf41af8870072/src/Ballpark.cpp#L2106-L2276)

## MOV-710 STOP damping rate

Each STOP tick multiplies the vertical velocity by the same extra factor before ordinary drag. With effective mass 2 kg, friction 1 kg/s, and both horizontal and vertical speed initially 10 m/s, horizontal speed ends at the same value across the three tick schedules within replay precision. Vertical speed falls further with each subdivision. Fitting a continuous extra damping rate to the one-second multiplier gives about 0.140229 s⁻¹. Lean proves that a single rate cannot produce the stored extra multiplier at both tested durations of 0.5 and 1 s. [STOP proof](RealPhysics/FrameAndOrbit.lean) · [Measured one-second outcomes](RESULTS.md#ten-mechanics-experiments) · [Destiny STOP operation](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/114e89fa584f0e1a81eb0a679aabf41af8870072/src/Ballpark.cpp#L1339-L1346)

**Change needed for time-based damping.** For velocity, apply `exp(−γ Δt)` using the one-second calibration. A continuous position model also needs to integrate the vertical velocity under the combined drag rate throughout the interval. Applying a kick first and then integrating ordinary drag has its own position trajectory. [Scalar tick](https://github.com/Eidosoma/exact-ballpark/blob/f34c1d14fc2c234d353bdb2578872cdb0478d19e/src/exact_ballpark/ref.py#L195-L212) · [Existing STOP subdivision proof](../ExactBallparkProofs/Movement/StopDistance.lean)

## MOV-720 circular reference speed

For a ship with effective mass 2 kg, friction 1 kg/s and requested cruise speed 20 m/s, the acceleration budget is 10 m/s². At centre distance 10 m, the new-style circular branch requests 10 m/s. At that speed, staying on the circle needs 10 m/s² inward plus 5 m/s² tangentially to replace drag. Their resultant exceeds the budget. The scalar replay reads the velocity sent to guidance and the actual capped thrust. The upstream source explicitly describes its reference as a curvature estimate that ignores friction. The sustained speed actually reached would require a trajectory measurement. [Destiny estimate](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/114e89fa584f0e1a81eb0a679aabf41af8870072/src/Ballpark.cpp#L1184-L1206) · [Numerical controls](mechanics_checks.py)

**Change needed for a sustainable target.** With a stationary centre, still medium, positive centre distance `R`, drag rate `β = k/M` and positive thrust budget `A`, cap the reference at the positive solution of `(v²/R)² + (βv)² = A²`. The resulting ceiling is 9.395649 m/s in the 10 m fixture. This policy would change guidance on that tick. A moving centre or spiral approach has a different force requirement. [Ceiling proof](RealPhysics/FrameAndOrbit.lean) · [Scalar circular guidance](https://github.com/Eidosoma/exact-ballpark/blob/f34c1d14fc2c234d353bdb2578872cdb0478d19e/src/exact_ballpark/modes.py#L509-L536)

## CMB-702 tracking axes

Use the same binary64 inputs in both calls, with sightline (3, 4, 12) m and relative velocity (0.03, 0.04, 0.12) m/s. Cycling both triples to (y, z, x) is an exact rotation of their stored components. The rounded projection gives a negative squared speed in the original order and positive zero in the rotated order. The exact stored-input result is positive and invariant. At tracking ratio 1 the scalar probability rounds to 1 in both frames. [Binary64 rotation witness](RealPhysics/TrackingAxes.lean) · [Real frame theorem](../ExactBallparkProofs/Physics/ContactFrames.lean) · [Earlier tracking analysis and exact reference](../DISCREPANCIES.md#cmb-702-tracking)

**Change needed for a frame-independent query.** Use the squared cross product over distance to the fourth, with compensated products or wider arithmetic for near-radial geometry. The checked fused-product control recovers the two small nonzero components on these inputs. A bitwise guarantee across all signed axis permutations also needs reproducible accumulation of squared components and distance. [Compensated control](../ExactBallparkProofs/Combat/TrackingMargin.lean) · [Destiny projection and square root](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/114e89fa584f0e1a81eb0a679aabf41af8870072/src/Ballpark.cpp#L5189-L5214)

## MOV-703 drag cutoff

For positive effective mass `M` and duration `t`, the source test `k < c M t` is exactly equivalent over the reals to `k t / M < c t²`. The decay magnitude `k t / M` is dimensionless. Giving the source literal `c` units of inverse seconds squared makes the source expression dimensionally consistent. A fixed dimensionless cutoff set to the same numerical value at one second makes a different choice at other durations for some inputs. [Typed proof](RealPhysics/TimeGuards.lean) · [Scalar integrator](https://github.com/Eidosoma/exact-ballpark/blob/f34c1d14fc2c234d353bdb2578872cdb0478d19e/src/exact_ballpark/ref.py#L177-L196) · [Destiny integrator](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/114e89fa584f0e1a81eb0a679aabf41af8870072/src/Ballpark.cpp#L751-L774)

Start from rest at the origin with `M = 1`, `k = 1e-10` and applied acceleration 1 m/s². At half a second the engine selects the ordinary formula. Cancellation in its rounded position gives about −413.70 m, while continuous drag gives about +0.125 m. At two seconds it selects the special branch and stores 0 m, while continuous drag gives about +2 m. The ideal positions use the stored drag rate and 70-digit arithmetic. The C++ and scalar positions match bit for bit. This distinguishes ordinary cancellation from the special branch’s first-order position approximation. [Replayed positions](RESULTS.md#mov-703-branch-decisions) · [Existing MOV-703 rounding case](../DISCREPANCIES.md#mov-703-position)

**Change needed to adopt this interpretation.** Choose the tolerance on the dimensionless exponent and an explicit position-error target. Accurate positions through this range would also require a stable small-exponent evaluation of the flow, including the acceleration term. Changing which branch wins can expose either source of position error. Any proposed policy needs validation across step lengths, especially at the threshold. [Source model for both branches](../ExactBallparkProofs/Movement/SpecialBranch.lean)
